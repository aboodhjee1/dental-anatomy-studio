# Dental Anatomy Studio — v0.4

A local, interactive 3D atlas for dental anatomy study: 22 skull bones, 8 muscles,
26 sourced nerve structures (13 nerve families, left and right), six salivary
glands, and two upper second molars (64 selectable meshes).

## Run locally

Open a terminal **in this folder** and run:

```powershell
npm start
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:8000. No install or build step is needed: Three.js 0.160.0
and its required add-ons are bundled under `vendor/three`. The app itself works
offline; external reference links need internet access.

If port 8000 is occupied, choose another port:

```powershell
$env:PORT = '8001'
npm start
```

Alternatively, `python -m http.server 8000 --bind 127.0.0.1` serves the same files.
Do not open `index.html` directly with a file:// URL. A WebGL-capable browser is
required. Development scripts/tests use Node.js 22.15 or newer.

## Study controls

- **Bone study / Dental nerves / Salivary glands / All layers:** presets for visibility and opacity.
  Dental nerves makes bone transparent and hides muscles.
- **Layer cards:** toggle an entire layer and adjust its opacity. A partly checked
  layer means only some of its structures are visible. Toggling a layer changes
  all its structure checkboxes together.
- **Search:** searches structure names, divisions, and study-note text. The small
  All/Bones/Muscles/Nerves buttons filter the list; they do not change visibility.
- **Select:** click/tap a structure or its name to reveal notes and highlight it.
  Selecting a hidden entry reveals it. The checkbox controls visibility independently.
- **Show only / Restore context:** temporarily isolate one structure, preserving
  your previous visibility choices. Right-click without dragging is a solo shortcut.
- **Front / Right / Left / Base:** anatomical camera views. Right and left refer
  to the subject. Drag rotates, scroll/pinch zooms, right-drag/two-finger drag pans.
- **Fit view / Zoom to structure:** frame all visible parts or the selected part.
- **Separate bones:** exploded view. Soft tissue and nerve paths remain assembled;
  use 0% to study their relationship with the bones.
- **Structure & landmark labels:** toggles immediately. Names up to 14 visible
  structures (select any structure to focus its label). Selecting the mandible
  shows four surface pointers for the left/right condylar heads and coronoid tips.
  These are visual localizations, not expert-validated measurements. Other fine
  bone landmarks remain in the notes. Labels follow visibility and separation.
- **Salivary glands:** focuses on the lower face with transparent bone and names
  the glands and upper second molars. Select a gland for notes and a duct diagram.
- **Duct openings in the mouth:** opens an enlarged, accessible teaching diagram;
  switch between Parotid, Submandibular and Sublingual. The drawings are not to
  scale and do not claim measured 3D duct trajectories.
- **Reset:** restore bone study, assembled position, front view, and labels off.
  Esc clears selection; / focuses search. Controls remain available on phones,
  where the structure browser and notes follow the model vertically.

## Dental nerve layer: scope and limitations

The selectable bilateral source meshes cover:

- V2: maxillary trunk, posterior/middle/anterior superior alveolar, greater palatine,
  and nasopalatine nerves.
- V3: mandibular, inferior alveolar, mental, lingual, long buccal, auriculotemporal,
  and nerve to mylohyoid.

The previous hand-authored curves were inaccurate and have been removed. Version
0.3 uses **actual BodyParts3D 4.3 atlas meshes**, downloaded directly from
`https://lifesciencedb.jp/bp3d/download.cgi` on 2026-09-07. Left and right use their
respective source objects. They are not generated by mirroring. No synthetic
paths, anatomical reshaping, or exaggerated tube thickness are applied.

The original skull comes from an older BodyParts3D version. A single uniform
scale (0.9441757368) and translation (-0.03864297, -14.7270949, 93.3948070 mm)
registers the new meshes to the skull. This was fitted against the mandible,
frontal bone, and both maxillae; RMS nearest-vertex differences range from 0.41 to
0.47 mm (95th percentiles 0.75–0.94 mm). These measurements establish agreement
between the reference bones; **they do not establish clinical nerve accuracy**.
The atlas is not patient-specific and may omit terminal branches or variations.

There are no independent infraorbital, lesser palatine, or incisive nerve meshes
in this selection. Their old invented objects have been removed. The source
maxillary trunk includes the continuing infraorbital course, described in its notes.
The middle superior alveolar nerve is variable and may be absent in an individual.

Source OBJ files, exact FJ/FMA identifiers, SHA-256 hashes, and original naming
are included in `sources/bodyparts3d/manifest.json`. Conversion retains the source
vertex count and triangle topology. `data/nerve_registration.json` records the
registration and measured residuals. The old source mirror only contained the
older skull dataset; the official 4.3 service includes these additional nerves.

Nerve study notes link to their references in the app. Main sources:

- [Maxillary nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542277/)
- [Mandibular nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507820/)
- [Maxilla](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538527/)
- [Palate](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557817/)
- [Lingual nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546652/)
- [Inferior alveolar nerve anatomy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564368/)
- [Teeth](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557543/)

Existing bone/muscle prose is inherited from the previous version and has not
undergone a comprehensive anatomical review. The muscle layer is a selected set,
not a complete collection of all muscles of mastication. Other cranial nerves,
autonomic pathways, teeth meshes, and a quiz system are possible future work.

## Project structure

- `index.html`, `css/`: responsive study interface.
- `js/app.js`: list, study notes, presets, and UI state synchronization.
- `js/viewer.js`: layer-independent rendering, picking, opacity, labels, and camera.
- `js/loader.js`: explicit content-schema loading and validation.
- `data/layers.json`: layer registrations, coordinate rotation, and preset settings.
- `data/*_content.json`: per-structure notes and mesh references.
- `data/nerve_registration.json`: common registration transform and reference-bone residuals.
- `sources/bodyparts3d/`: original nerve/reference OBJs and provenance manifest.
- `meshes/`: bone GLBs, muscle STLs, and generated nerve GLBs.
- `scripts/import_nerves.py`: reproducible OBJ-to-GLB conversion with the recorded transform.
- `scripts/register_nerves.py`: reproduce the fit from the four reference bones (requires numpy).
- `scripts/serve.mjs`: local HTTP server, bound to loopback only.
- `tests/`: asset validation and interaction regression tests.

Add a layer with a mesh folder, content JSON (`parts` map), and registry entry.
All layers load even when initially hidden. Part IDs are namespaced by layer.
Colours, opacity defaults, and whether a layer separates are registration options.
The source meshes use +Z up; the registry rotates the entire assembly to +Y up.

Rebuild the sourced meshes and check the project (Python + numpy required for conversion only):

```powershell
npm run import:nerves
npm test
```

The tests cover every mesh reference and binary geometry, visibility-aware picking,
annotation exclusion, drag gestures, solo/restore state, shader transparency,
exploded labels and resource cleanup, and layer content normalization.

## Landmark placement review (2026-09-07)

The previous review did not verify any anatomical landmark coordinates.
Legacy `anchor` arrays are archived in `sources/landmarks` and never rendered or
snapped to vertices. Surface proximity does not establish anatomical identity.
The source overview does not establish the app's guessed landmark coordinates:
https://lifesciencedb.jp/bp3d/info_en/index.html

New records require `coordinateSpace: "part-local"`, a finite three-number
`position`, optional `offset` in the same frame, and `evidence` containing
`status: "reviewed"`, `mesh` matching the loaded mesh URL, `source`, `description`
of mesh-specific localization, and `reviewer`. Coordinates precede the assembly
rotation and explode transforms. Metadata records review; it does not prove
anatomical truth. Review the exact mesh/version in multiple views before adding
points, and re-review placements if assets change.

The v0.4 update adds four visually localized mandible surface points with exact
vertex indices and mesh hashes. This documents mesh inspection, not independent
anatomical validation. All other fine landmark placements remain outstanding.
Whole-structure name labels identify the source mesh only. Muscle coverage is unchanged.


## Salivary sources and limitations

The submandibular and sublingual glands and two upper second molars are official
BodyParts3D 4.3 surfaces, with their independent left/right FJ identifiers and
SHA-256 hashes recorded in `sources/salivary/manifest.json`. They use the same
uniform skull registration as the nerves, without anatomical reshaping.

The two parotids come from the SPL Head and Neck Atlas (Marianna Jakab and Ron
Kikinis, Surgical Planning Laboratory, Brigham and Women's Hospital):
https://www.openanatomy.org/atlas-pages/atlas-spl-head-and-neck.html
Their VTK surfaces were converted to GLB and approximately aligned to this
skull through one similarity transform fitted to the mandible. This is a
**different specimen**: sampled nearest-vertex RMS is about 2.99 mm and p95 is
5.65 mm. These residuals describe mandibular alignment only, not gland accuracy.
The app identifies this limitation in the salivary view and parotid notes.
Original VTK surfaces, the reference mandible and Slicer license are included
under `sources/salivary`. `data/parotid_registration.json` stores the transform.

Ducts and mucosal openings are provided as separate educational diagrams in
`js/salivary.js`, not fabricated 3D atlas structures. The diagrams show the
parotid papilla opposite the upper second molar; paired sublingual caruncles
beside the lingual frenulum; and Rivinus openings along the sublingual folds.
Bartholin duct anatomy is variable. Sources are linked within the app:
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538325/
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9640814/

Rebuild the eight salivary/reference GLBs with `python scripts/import_salivary.py`
(numpy required). `npm test` checks provenance, source vertex counts, landmark
vertex/hash identity, transforms, labels, picking, and existing nerve geometry.
Browser checks exercise labels, gland selection, diagrams and responsive layout.
No APIs or credentials are needed by the app.


## Dentistry study controls (0.5)

Choose **Dentistry only** in the sidebar to filter the model and list to
52 source structures: 10 bones, 8 oral/chewing muscles, 26 dental nerves,
6 salivary glands and 2 upper second molars. Retained bones are the mandible,
maxillae, palatine bones, temporal bones (TMJ), zygomatic bones and sphenoid
(pterygoid support and dental nerve pathways). Whole source meshes are
retained; this is a focused study selection, not a claim that other bones
have no relevance to any dental specialty. **All anatomy** restores the full
catalog. Scope persists across presets, layer toggles and reset.

Hover or right-click a visible structure for **Hide this**, **Show only this**
and **Study notes**. Touch and keyboard users can select a list row and use
Hide or Show only in the notes panel. Undo hide restores the most recently
hidden structure; Restore other structures exits solo while preserving hidden
choices. Switching study presets clears the undo history.

Enable labels and select the mandible, a maxilla, a palatine bone or sphenoid
to show the available dental landmark pointers (24 total). These are visually
localized study pointers, not expert-validated clinical measurements.
